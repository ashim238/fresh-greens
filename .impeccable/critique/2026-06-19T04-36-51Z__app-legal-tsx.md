## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**No slop detected.** Screen passes AI-slop test cleanly. No gradient text, glassmorphism, eyebrow labels, identical card grid, decoration-for-decoration. The grouped-gray + white-card register is HIG-native. Token discipline consistent throughout — every color, spacing value, radius, and shadow pulled from `theme/`. Copy is purposefully plain and human ("We wrote it specifically to match what Fresh Greens actually does, not as a generic template").

One minor brand-register flag: `EffectiveDate` renders raw `2026-05-31` ISO date string. Technically correct, but the tone is form-document rather than companion. Not slop — just a small register inconsistency.

## Cognitive Load

**Intrinsic load is high by necessity.** Legal/disclosure content carries inherent density. Screen correctly offloads through: chunked H2 sub-sections within each tab, `relaxedLineHeight` on paragraph and bullet body copy (1.6× ratio — the right call), and a BoldInline pattern in Terms surfacing "What this is not" statements immediately.

**Extraneous load sources:**

1. **H2 and H3 are visually indistinguishable except by color.** Both use `typography.bodyEmphasized` (17pt/600). Only distinction: `colors.black` vs `colors.labelSecondary`. A user without normal color vision sees two visually identical heading tiers. Hierarchy collapses.

2. **The Limitations section is third.** For target user, Limitations is most cognitively important section (tells them what the app cannot do during a safety event). Placing it last forces user to either read linearly through dense policy or know to jump directly.

3. **Bullet dot color mismatch.** `bulletDot` is `colors.labelSecondary`, `bulletText` is `colors.black`. Subtle 2-tone bullet creates tiny attention split on every list item.

## Emotional Journey

**Entry:** User arrives from /menu or settings prompt. Screen opens on Privacy — reasonable default but not the section most likely to be the reason they came. SettingsHeader "Privacy & Terms" title accurate but slightly clinical.

**Mid-read:** Privacy copy is excellent — direct, specific, trust-building. The TL;DR section is the strongest structural decision on the screen: front-loads the user's most anxious question ("does this app track me?") with five plain-language answers before detailed sections.

**Tab navigation:** Active-pill feedback immediate and visually clear. But after tapping "Limitations" and scrolling, if user scrolls back up into Terms content, pill still says "Limitations." Interface is lying about where they are. For trust-sensitive screen (legal disclosures for safety app), navigation indicator that lies is particularly damaging.

**The Limitations section** is emotionally the most important moment on this screen for primary persona. "Fresh Greens is a prototype. Use it like a prototype" is honest, grounded, calibrated — earns trust precisely because it refuses to oversell. Emotional arc on that section is strong.

**Exit:** Close-to-/home affordance correct. Users shouldn't be stranded in settings register after reading legal.

## What's Working

**1. TL;DR section in Privacy is a standout decision.** Leading the Privacy section with five scannable bullets immediately answers the user's highest-anxiety question before the detailed policy. Doesn't show up in design audits but carries a disproportionate share of trust-building work.

**2. Token discipline is exemplary.** Every style value in the 452-line file traces to a theme import. No inline hex, no hard-coded spacing, no rogue `borderRadius: 20`. The `relaxedLineHeight` application on paragraph and bullet copy is the correct call for a long-form reading surface.

**3. The Limitations section copy is the best writing in the app.** Plain, specific, radically honest about prototype status without being self-deprecating. "Use it the way you would use a thesis project" is exactly the Steady Companion register applied to a difficult disclosure. Treats the user as an adult.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Tab pills have 28pt painted height — 36% below the 44pt tap-target floor**
- What: `styles.tab` uses `paddingVertical: spacing.xs` (4pt). With `subheadlineEmphasized` at 20pt lineHeight: 4 + 20 + 4 = 28pt painted height. Active pill is the primary navigation control — sub-44pt on the painted visual, not just hit area.
- Why it matters: `.cursorrules` explicit: "44×44 pt minimum on both axes — on the visual, not just the hit area." `hitSlop` is "forgiveness on top of compliance, never the compliance mechanism." 28pt painted target is a real miss-tap risk in settings context.
- Fix: Replace `paddingVertical: spacing.xs` with `minHeight: 44` (or apply `tapTarget44`). Adjust `paddingVertical` to `spacing.md` (16pt) to produce 52pt painted height proportional at subheadline size.

**[P1] No scroll-spy: active tab doesn't track scroll position**
- What: `activeSection` set only in `jumpTo()`. Once user scrolls manually after jumping (or scrolls past section boundary), active tab frozen on last-tapped value. UI claims user is in "Privacy" while they read "Terms."
- Why it matters: Visibility of System Status failure (H1). Users frequently scroll to browse after initial jump. Frozen indicator does opposite of helping. For trust-sensitive screen, a navigation indicator that lies is particularly damaging.
- Fix: Add `onScroll` + `scrollEventThrottle={16}` to ScrollView. Track scroll position against `anchorOffsets.current` to set `activeSection` as user scrolls. Section whose anchor offset is closest-to-but-not-greater-than current scroll Y is active.

**[P1] `accessibilityRole="button"` on tabs — VoiceOver reads as buttons, not tab group**
- What: Tab Pressables declare `accessibilityRole="button"`. Tab row container has no role. VoiceOver announces "Privacy, button" — won't communicate that these are tab selectors in a group.
- Why it matters: `accessibilityRole="tab"` combined with parent `accessibilityRole="tablist"` is correct iOS semantic. VoiceOver's "tab" role announces "Privacy, tab, 1 of 3, selected" — gives count, position, state in one announcement.
- Fix: Add `accessibilityRole="tablist"` to `styles.tabRow` View. Change `accessibilityRole="button"` → `accessibilityRole="tab"` on each Pressable. Update `accessibilityLabel` from `Jump to ${s.label}` to just `s.label`.

**[P2] H2 and H3 visually identical — heading hierarchy collapses without color**
- What: Both `styles.h2` and `styles.h3` use `typography.bodyEmphasized` (17pt / 600 / -0.43 tracking). Only distinction: `colors.black` vs `colors.labelSecondary`. No size difference, no weight difference.
- Why it matters: Fails WCAG 1.4.1 (Use of Color) for hierarchy communication. For colorblind user, H2 and H3 look identical. Structural hierarchy of document becomes ambiguous.
- Fix: Give H3 visually distinct style. Options: (a) drop H3 to `typography.subheadlineEmphasized` (15pt / 600) — one size step down; (b) keep 17pt but use `typography.subheadlineRegular` so H3 is lighter weight, not just dimmer.

**[P2] Limitations tab buried third — most critical trust content for primary persona is hardest to reach**
- What: Privacy / Terms / Limitations tab order places the section that most directly answers "can I trust what this app tells me during a safety event?" behind two dense policy sections.
- Why it matters: Thesis claim is that this app serves Black drivers in charged moments. "Your trusted contact does not receive an SMS or live-location feed from Fresh Greens" is the most operationally significant disclosure. Placing it last prioritizes compliance-first reading order over user-need-first.
- Fix: (a) Reorder tabs to Limitations / Privacy / Terms; or (b) Add persistent callout at top of contentCard with tap-to-jump link to Limitations: "This is a thesis prototype. Some features are simulated."

**[P3] EffectiveDate duplicated in Privacy and Terms — Limitations has none**
- What: `<EffectiveDate />` appears after Privacy `SectionHeader` and after Terms `SectionHeader`, but not after Limitations `SectionHeader`. Limitations is a material disclosure document.
- Why it matters: Inconsistency — user reading Limitations has no date reference. Sets up maintenance trap.
- Fix: Add `<EffectiveDate />` after Limitations `SectionHeader`. Extract date to named constant at top of file.

## Persona Red Flags

**Sam (accessibility):**
`dynamicType()` wrapper correct, `relaxedLineHeight` on body/bullets is the right call. However: VoiceOver reads tabs as "buttons" not "tabs" — Sam gets no count or position context. Sam also gets no announcement when scroll crosses section boundary — frozen `activeSection` means VoiceOver's `accessibilityState={{ selected }}` stale moment they scroll past section. At AX5, 28pt painted tab height even more undersized relative to enlarged text.

**Casey (distracted mobile):**
Casey will tap "Limitations" (or try — 28pt target), land near that section, scroll through dense copy. Once Casey's eye catches something interesting and they scroll manually, active tab freezes. They lose track of where they are. Frozen pill is the single most disorienting thing on this screen for casual reader. Casey unlikely to discover re-tapping active tab re-jumps. Casey will scroll up and down hunting rather than using tabs as primary navigation.

**Black driver assessing safety in a charged moment:**
Arrives on /legal to understand what app does and does not do. Most critical disclosure is in Limitations (third tab). If they land here during/after safety event, reading under stress. Frozen active tab will read as broken interface — broken interface on safety-critical screen erodes trust in app's entire safety infrastructure. `relaxedLineHeight` on body copy right call (same logic as pulled-over guidance). Limitations ordering and scroll-spy gap most likely to cause this user to leave with incomplete understanding.

## Minor Observations

- `/recordings` path reference in plain text reads as developer path, not UI affordance. Should link to actual screen or name the surface ("the Recordings screen").
- ISO date format on `EffectiveDate` — "May 31, 2026" reads warmer, consistent with iOS Settings.
- `sectionHeader` `marginTop: spacing.lg` on first section creates 48pt top whitespace combined with card padding.
- `h2` and `h3` `marginTop` not suppressed when immediately following `SectionHeader` or `EffectiveDate` — 40pt between effective date and first H2.
- `BoldInline` typed as `string` — can't render any inline styled children. `children: React.ReactNode` would be more flexible.
- `showsVerticalScrollIndicator={false}` on main ScrollView — legal content is long-form; scroll indicator is wayfinding cue especially relevant given lack of scroll-spy.

## Questions to Consider

1. What is the path to /legal from inside the app? If only from /menu settings, the "charged moment" persona reaching it during a traffic stop is unlikely — but if there's a link from pulled-over or share-location flows, the tab order and scroll-spy priority both increase significantly.
2. Should Limitations be a first-class pre-onboarding disclosure rather than a settings-buried tab? Thesis claim that "Fresh Greens is a prototype" is arguably important enough to surface at first launch.
3. What happens when user re-taps the active tab? Currently `jumpTo` re-executes — scrolls back to section top. Is that intended? Undiscoverable, no animation affordance.
4. Is the `anchorOffsets` scroll-coordinate approach robust enough? On short device (SE) or at large Dynamic Type, the overshoot might be less imperceptible.
