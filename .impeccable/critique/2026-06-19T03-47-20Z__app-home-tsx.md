## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Not AI slop. Human-designed, with a point of view.** All absolute bans clear: no gradient text (the daylight strip is a 4pt LinearGradient bar, not text), no glassmorphism-as-default (solid white sheets; only purposeful scrim/fill translucency), no identical card grids (cards differentiate by topline variant, photo state, per-category glyph), one eyebrow not per-section, no side-stripe borders. The reserved-color discipline holds (orange = hazard only, red absent from this screen), and the "Along this route:" briefing frame is a deliberate tonal decision a generator wouldn't reach. Deterministic detector N/A (React Native — no HTML/CSS surface to scan). The one generic element: the first-run coaching overlay.

## Overall Impression

The bones are strong and the thesis is legible in the UI — auditable safety reasoning is expressed as actual components (the hazard/safe chip briefing, the all-clear chip). The gaps are execution-level, not architectural. The single biggest opportunity: the safety-check failure path ("Couldn't check route") is a silent dead end in a safety app — the worst possible place for one.

## What's Working

1. **Reserved-color discipline is load-bearing and it holds.** Orange appears only on hazard chips; red is absent from the screen entirely; greens carry every affirmation. The all-clear chip means something precisely because orange appears nowhere decorative.
2. **The chip architecture is "safety briefing, not alarm system."** "Along this route:" framing + showing safe-zone chips alongside hazard chips surfaces the reasoning, not just the verdict — the thesis claim expressed as a component.
3. **Consistent haptic grammar.** selectionAsync (navigation) / impact Light (placement) / notification Success (commitment) — three registers for three semantic weights, reinforcing "Steady Companion" without visual weight.

## Priority Issues

- **[P0] "Couldn't check route" is a silent dead end.** `RouteZonesFetchFailedChip` renders a gray pill with no action and is `accessibilityElementsHidden`. A safety app that says it can't verify the route and offers nothing is a trust rupture — most acute for the core persona. **Fix:** make the chip a retry button (bump a retry counter to re-trigger the corridor fetch), `accessibilityRole="button"` + "Tap to retry" hint, remove the a11y hide.

- **[P1] First-run coaching overlay is generic and off-brand.** Full-screen scrim + white card + 3 generic bullets ("Colored overlays show lighting…"), dismiss is an 11pt `caption2Regular` caption (below the WCAG 1.4.4 floor the codebase's own tokens flag). The one moment to invoke the Green Book lineage, and it says nothing of the kind. **Fix:** explicit "Got it" button in the card (footnoteEmphasized, wiltedgreen), rewrite copy in the app's voice with community/heritage context.

- **[P1] Destination title as implicit save toggle is discoverable only by accident.** The destination name text is a Pressable that saves a regular destination, but the only affordance is a bookmark glyph that appears *after* saving. **Fix:** show a hollow bookmark icon pre-tap (hollow → filled on save), mirroring iOS Maps/Reminders.

- **[P1] Working-memory cliff on hazard-chip tap.** Tapping a hazard chip flies the camera, opens the detail card, and hides the route sheet — destination, ETA, route option all vanish while reading about one zone. **Fix:** show destination name + "← Back to route" in the RouteHazardDetailCard header.

- **[P2] Route-cycling count is VoiceOver-only.** Caret chevrons appear with multiple routes but no sighted "1 of 3" indicator. **Fix:** add a dot strip or "1 of 3" label between the chevrons (caption1Regular / labelTertiary).

- **[P2] `daylightArrivalHint` at caption2 (11pt) for informational content.** The arrival-light hint is one of the few redundant non-color cues for colorblind drivers, set below the readable floor. **Fix:** bump to footnoteRegular (13pt).

## Persona Red Flags

**Sam (accessibility):** Route loading/fail/all-clear chips are `accessibilityElementsHidden` — the fail chip gives no role/hint/recovery. Scroll-spy active chip uses color-only selection for sighted users (state is set for VoiceOver, but no non-color visual cue). Star rating ~2.5:1 on fillsPrimary (documented exception).

**Casey (distracted one-handed mobile):** "Show all categories" can land in the stretch zone after scrolling. Drag handle doubles as expand toggle — accidental collapse risk on a one-handed flick from the FAB column. No explicit state-persistence guard if the OS reclaims memory mid-route-review.

**Black driver assessing safety in a charged moment:** The "Couldn't check route" dead end is the most dangerous element for this persona. "Along this route:" briefing header is small (13pt labelTertiary) — scanning at a light, a driver may fixate on ETA and read orange chips as raw alarm without the briefing frame. Routes with no safe chips show only negative data — consider always naming at least one affirmative signal ("no community flags"). The coaching overlay is the one moment to speak to the thesis and it's silent.

## Minor Observations

- Three modal-dismiss patterns (pill X / FAB cancel / scrim tap) is one too many — converging would tighten consistency.
- Browse sheet density: ~9 named sections per scroll reads dense even when populated; curation could beat the collapse/expand toggle.
- "Along this route:" header weight could anchor the chip row better at footnoteEmphasized.

## Questions to Consider

- What does a confident *recovery* from a failed safety check look like — not a gray badge, but a path?
- Could the first-run overlay carry the thesis (Green Book lineage) instead of generic map-legend copy?
- Should at least one affirmative signal always appear, so the reasoning is auditable even on a poorly-lit route?
